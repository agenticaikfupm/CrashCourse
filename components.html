Narration:

What is an AI agent?

A chatbot answers a question. An agent works toward a goal: it can take several steps, call tools, check results, and decide what to do next. In most modern agents a foundation model (a large language or reasoning model) does the reasoning, and ordinary software provides everything else: memory, tools, rules, and logging.

A useful mental model

Agent = model + instructions + memory + tools + a loop, wrapped in safety controls and human oversight.

The nine components at a glance

Agent components, their purpose, and an everyday analogy
ComponentPurposeEveryday analogy
PerceptionTurn raw inputs into structured observationsReading and understanding an email
ReasoningInterpret the goal and decide what mattersThinking through options
PlanningOrder the steps needed to reach the goalWriting a to-do list
ActionExecute a step that affects the worldMaking the phone call
ToolsExtend abilities with APIs, search, code, solversUsing a calculator or a map app
MemoryKeep context now and knowledge over timeNotes and personal experience
Feedback and learningCompare results with goals and improveChecking whether the plan worked
Safety and governanceRules, limits, logging, and evaluationCompany policies and audits
Human oversightApprove, correct, or stop the agentA manager who signs off on big decisions

Key takeaways

  • The foundation model is only one component. Reliability comes from the parts around it.
  • Tools give agents real capabilities, so actions are where safety checks and approvals belong.
  • Memory and feedback turn a one-shot answer into a system that improves over a task.
