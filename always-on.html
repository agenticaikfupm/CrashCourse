Module 4

Always-on AI Agents

Most AI tools wait for you to ask. Always-on agents keep working in the background: they watch for events, follow routines, remember your goals, and bring you finished work, while asking for approval before anything consequential.

Narration:

From reactive assistants to always-on agents

In the earlier modules, an agent starts when you send a request and stops when it answers. An always-on agent is persistent: it has a lasting identity and memory, it can be woken up by things other than your messages, and it can work for hours or days on your goals. Several companies launched products of this kind in 2026, often describing them as AI teammates or personal agents.

How an always-on agent works

The products below differ in detail but share the same building blocks. Press Play to follow one cycle.

Triggers

Schedules (routines), events in connected apps, your messages, and read-only background checks for ways to help.

Persistence

Long-term memory of goals and preferences, and a workspace that survives between tasks, such as a dedicated cloud computer.

Control

Permission rules, approval requests, an activity log you can inspect, and the ability to pause or redirect the agent.

Examples

Descriptions below are summarized from each company's official product page at the time of writing. Features, availability, and pricing change often, so check the linked pages for current details.

OpenAI

dots

Announced September 29, 2026, as "always-on agents" in ChatGPT, powered by GPT-6 Astra. Each dot has its own cloud computer and browser, connects to apps through plugins, and learns preferences from feedback. You can message or call it in ChatGPT, Slack, or Teams.

  • Proactive: "proactive research" in the background, using connected apps through read-only tools.
  • Control: Custom Rules to allow, require approval for, or block actions; an Activity View; sensitive tasks such as changing a password always stay with you.
  • Teams: "specialist dots" with their own identity for organizational roles, starting with enterprise pilots.

Introducing dots

xAI

Grok Bot

Described as "AI teammates" that sign in to your tools, use them as you would, and return finished work. You give tasks from the desktop app (macOS or Windows) or the iOS app, and can run several Bots in parallel, around the clock.

  • Proactive: routines on a schedule; you can show a Bot a workflow once and it saves it as a routine.
  • Persistence: Bots share one persistent cloud computer per user and keep memory that improves over time.
  • Control: Bots come back when approval is needed, and sensitive actions can go through an automatic review first.

Grok Bot

Instinct

Instinct

A personal assistant with no new app to learn: you text it or call it. The company says it understands what you are working on and can use a phone and a computer the way you do, with examples such as ordering groceries, booking a train, and sending flowers.

  • Interface: text messages and phone calls.
  • Note: the product page does not describe how background work, approvals, or permissions are handled.

instinct.com

Meta

Muse

Meta's "personal AI agent." You chat with it in the Muse app on mobile or Mac, or in WhatsApp. It connects to apps such as email and calendar, and can build its own tools when a task needs one.

  • Proactive: turns a goal into an action plan, tracks progress, and suggests ideas.
  • Persistence: Muse Secure VM, a persistent, isolated Linux computer with a browser that you and the agent share.
  • Control: approval before actions such as sending emails or making purchases, a complete audit trail, a credential store the agent cannot read, and one-time card numbers at checkout.

Opportunities and risks

Opportunities

  • Work continues while you focus elsewhere
  • Problems are noticed early, sometimes before you ask
  • Routine processes run reliably on a schedule

Risks

  • Mistakes can happen while nobody is watching
  • Broad access to email, files, and accounts raises privacy and security stakes
  • Prompt injection through the content the agent reads
  • Too many notifications and approvals lead to rubber-stamping

Good practice

  • Start with read-only access and add permissions gradually
  • Require approval for anything that sends, pays, or deletes
  • Review the activity log regularly
  • See Module 10 for governance controls
