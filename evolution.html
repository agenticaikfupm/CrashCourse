Interactive timeline

Use the mouse, or focus the timeline and press the Left and Right arrow keys. The years follow the course slides and mark when each wave became prominent. Earlier research papers often came first; for example, the first Vision-Language-Action models appeared in 2023.

  1. 2014, GANs: generator and discriminator networks compete to create realistic content.
  2. 2017, Transformers: attention-based architecture behind modern language and vision models.
  3. 2020, LLMs: large next-token predictors that learn tasks from examples in the prompt.
  4. 2022, ChatGPT: instruction-following chat assistants for everyone.
  5. 2023, MLLMs: models that understand images and documents as well as text.
  6. 2024, AI Agents: LLMs in a loop with tools and memory.
  7. 2025, Agentic AI: autonomous, long-running, multi-agent systems with oversight.
  8. 2026, VLA, SLA & MHS: Vision-Language-Action and Sensor-Language-Action models, plus the Model Hardware Standard that lets agents operate physical devices.
  9. Next, Physical AI: agents that perceive and act in the real world.

Frontier models along the way

The course slide also tracks two frontier model families. Notice the trend in the highlights: from chat, to reasoning and multimodality, to computer use and long-running, always-on agents.

The big picture

Generate

GANs and Transformers taught models to create content and to understand long context.

Understand and talk

LLMs, chat assistants, and multimodal models made language and images a universal interface.

Act

Agents, VLA and SLA models, hardware standards, and Physical AI connect that understanding to tools, software, and machines.

Try it: how a language model generates text

Every LLM, from GPT-3 to today's frontier models, generates text with the same loop: predict a probability for each possible next token, choose one, append it, and repeat. The first example builds a tiny model so you can see every number. Change the temperature and run it again: higher values make the output more random.

The second example uses real pretrained models from Hugging Face: a small text generator and CLIP, a vision-language model that scores how well an image matches each text label. It needs to download models, so it runs in Google Colab.

Key takeaways

  • Each wave added a capability: generation, context, language, instruction following, perception, action, autonomy, and embodiment.
  • Agents are not a new kind of model. They are systems built around models, adding tools, memory, planning, and feedback.
  • As agents gain physical and real-world reach, governance and human oversight matter more (see Module 10).
